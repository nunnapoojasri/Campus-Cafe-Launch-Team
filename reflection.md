# Reflection on agent collaboration

The division of work makes the brief more useful than asking one model to research, calculate, persuade, and approve its own argument. The Market Researcher is instructed to attach source URLs and distinguish national evidence from local demand. The Financial Analyst receives the assumptions explicitly and has a calculator tool, which makes the break-even chain easier to inspect than a rounded result alone. The writer can then focus on audience and clarity rather than inventing evidence or doing arithmetic. Finally, the Skeptical Reviewer has both the draft and the underlying evidence and calculations, so it can challenge unsupported claims and missing costs before the final version is produced.

That separation improves traceability, but it does not make the result independently verified. Search results may be stale or weakly relevant, model-generated citations still need checking, and a correct break-even formula cannot make uncertain cost assumptions reliable. The sample brief therefore treats national coffee consumption only as category context, does not claim that it proves student demand, labels menu and cost inputs as assumptions, and recommends local price and demand tests before committing funds. In practice, the reviewer adds the most value when it has the source material and the calculation details—not just the writer's polished draft.

The exercise lab makes the effects of coordination visible instead of treating the workflow as a black box. Removing the analyst's research context isolates whether that handoff changes an assumption-driven calculation. The Sustainability Officer can block or qualify green claims before drafting. Sequential and hierarchical runs can be compared for their outputs, elapsed time, and token usage, while skeptical and enthusiastic reviewer runs illustrate how tone may affect risk scrutiny. Injecting a temporary calculator outage tests whether the analyst retries; a persistent outage should lead to an explicit limitation, not invented arithmetic. These comparisons are demonstrations rather than controlled experiments: local-model outputs vary, and shorter or faster output is not necessarily better. A human decision-maker should still verify sources and obtain real quotes.
